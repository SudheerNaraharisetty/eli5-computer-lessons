Maya's shop has three jobs. Show the price, take the payment, print the receipt. Each one works out the total with the very same line.

Then comes a new rule. Orders of twenty dollars or more get ten percent off. Maya writes the rule into the first job. Then the second. Then the third.

But one copy came out slightly different. A customer orders exactly twenty dollars. The screen says nineteen sixty two. The card is charged twenty one eighty.

One rule, written three times, is three chances to get it wrong.

The fix. Pull the three copies into one function, called final total. Each job just asks it for the answer.

Next time the rule changes, Maya changes it once. And all three jobs agree.

That is dry. Don't repeat yourself. Give every rule one home.
