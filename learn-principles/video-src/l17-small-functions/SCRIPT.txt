Every evening, Maya's cookie shop runs one job. It is called close shop.

It started small. Now it is forty-two lines long. Count the till. Find the unsold cookies. Mark them half price. Back up the sales. Lock up. All in one long scroll.

Then a customer complains. Day-old cookies should be half price. One dollar fifty. The tag says two dollars fifty.

Where is the mistake? Maya starts at the top and reads. Line after line. Sum after sum. Twenty-two lines down, she finds it. One wrong character.

She fixes it. Then she fixes the real problem. Each chunk is cut out into its own small function, and named after its comment.

Now close shop is just five steps. It reads like a list.

Next time something breaks, Maya reads five names, opens one, and sees the whole thing at once.

That is the idea of small functions. Each function does one thing, and is small enough to see whole.
