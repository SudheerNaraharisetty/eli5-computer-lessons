Maya's loyalty card holds a customer's points. A free cookie costs thirty points.

Three places hand out free cookies. The till, the website, and phone orders.

Each one does the same thing. It asks the card, how many points? Then it decides for itself. Then it takes the points.

It looks polite. But count the rule. It lives in three places, and none of them is the card.

Then Maya starts a Tuesday offer. Twenty points. She updates the till and the website. She misses the phone.

It is Tuesday. Ana has twenty four points. At the till, she gets her cookie. On the phone, she is told no. Same card, same day, two answers.

The fix. Move the rule inside the card. Now nobody asks and decides. Each place just tells the card: redeem a free cookie. The card checks its own points, and answers yes or no.

That is tell, don't ask. Let the one who holds the data make the decision.
