Maya's checkout has one socket for payments. Whatever plugs in makes three promises. Never crash. Answer yes or no. And take exactly the amount asked.

Four payments already plug in, and each one keeps the promises.

Then Maya adds a fifth. The gift voucher. Same shape, so it plugs straight in.

Ben pays for a twenty six dollar cake with a twenty dollar gift voucher. It does not answer no. It raises an error, and the till crashes.

Ana buys six dollars of cookies. It answers yes, and quietly takes all twenty.

Put every plug on a test bench. Same three checks. The gift voucher fails two.

So Maya fixes the plug, not the checkout. Not enough? Answer no. Enough? Take exactly six, and leave fourteen.

Now all five pass. The checkout never has to ask which one it got.

That is Liskov substitution. A stand-in must keep every promise of the thing it replaces.
