Maya's shop has four ways to pay. Card. Cash. Phone. And gift voucher.

She writes one big list that every one of them must sign. Pay. Refund. Print a slip.

The card can do all three. But cash has no slip printer. Neither has the phone. And a gift voucher cannot refund, or print. The list makes them tick those boxes anyway. Four fake ticks.

Sam builds a refund button. A customer returns a twenty dollar cake, paid by voucher. The screen says refunded. The customer gets nothing.

Then Maya adds tips to the list. Only the card can take a tip. But all four have to sign again.

The fix. Cut the big list into small ones. Each way to pay wears only the badges it has earned.

Now the refund button asks for one badge. The voucher is not even offered. And the tip badge goes to the card alone.

That is interface segregation. Small, focused lists, so nobody signs for what they cannot do.
