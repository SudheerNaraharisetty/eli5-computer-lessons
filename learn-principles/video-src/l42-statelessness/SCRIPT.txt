Maya's website keeps every visitor's basket in its own memory. Like a clerk with a private notepad.

Ben adds two cookies. The clerk writes it down.

Then the program restarts. The notepad is wiped. Ben's basket is empty.

For the Christmas rush, Maya runs two copies of the program. Two clerks. Two notepads.

Ben adds two cookies at window A. His next click lands on window B. Empty. Back to A. Two cookies. His basket flickers.

Nothing is broken. Each copy just has its own private memory.

So throw the notepads away. Put one shared cabinet behind every window, and give Ben a basket number. Seventy-three oh five.

Now every request carries that number. Any clerk pulls the same card, changes it, puts it back, and forgets.

Swap a clerk mid-order. Nothing is lost. Run five copies. Every window gives the same answer.

That is statelessness. The program remembers nothing between requests. The memory lives in one shared place.
