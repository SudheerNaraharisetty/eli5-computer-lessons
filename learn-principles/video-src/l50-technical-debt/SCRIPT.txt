Two weeks before Christmas, Maya wants to sell hampers on her website. By Friday.

The proper way takes five days. So she takes a shortcut. She copies the order code, types the prices in, and skips the tests. One day. And she leaves a note. Fix in January.

Three hundred hampers sold. A good decision. She borrowed four days.

But January is busy. And a loan charges interest.

February. A change to orders must be made twice. She misses one. A day lost. March. A simple discount takes three days, not one. April. Sam loses a day, puzzled by two order systems.

By May, the interest has passed the loan. And it keeps growing.

So Maya pays it off. Five days, done properly. Now every change is made once.

An old shortcut, in a report nobody touches? No interest. Leave it.

That is technical debt. A shortcut is a loan. Take it on purpose, write it down, and pay it back where you work most.
