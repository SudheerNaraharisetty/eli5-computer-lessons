Christmas pre-orders must open in three weeks. But Maya's four developers still have six weeks of work left.

So Maya does the math. Hire four more. Twice the people, half the time. Three weeks.

Week one. The newcomers must learn the code before they can help. And Sam and Tom stop building, to teach them.

Week two. Count who has to talk to whom. Four people make six pairs. Eight people make twenty-eight. Meetings grow. Two people clash in one file. A newcomer breaks the tax.

And some work cannot be split. A cake needs its hour in the oven, however many bakers watch.

Maya's plan said week three. With no new people, week six. With them, week seven.

The better move? Cut the list. Ten tasks are enough to open pre-orders, and four people can finish those in time. And if you add people, add them early, with pieces of their own.

That is Brooks's law. Adding people to a late project makes it later.
