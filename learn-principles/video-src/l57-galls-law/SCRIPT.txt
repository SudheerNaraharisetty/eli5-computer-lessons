Maya's company plans one grand new system. Maya two point oh. Ordering, kitchen screens, delivery, loyalty points, stock, accounts, and a phone app. All designed up front, in a sixty page plan. Switch it all on in month nine.

For nine months, each team builds its own part. No real customer touches any of it.

Switch-on day. Orders vanish. Loyalty points double. The app cannot log in. Hundreds of guesses meet reality at once, and nobody can tell which one broke what. That night, they switch back.

Second try. Week one. Customers can order one cookie, and the kitchen sees it. That is all. Two wrong guesses show up. Each is fixed in a day, because only one thing was new.

Then one piece at a time. Delivery. Loyalty. The app. Each one works before the next goes on.

Count the working pieces. One line sat at zero for nine months. The other climbs, step by step.

That is Gall's law. Complex systems that work grow from simple ones that worked. So get the smallest thing working first.
