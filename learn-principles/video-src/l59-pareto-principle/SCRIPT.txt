Last month, Maya's company got two hundred complaints.

Maya's plan: fix them in the order they arrived. After one week, two causes are fixed. Together, they caused just fourteen.

Sam counts first. He sorts the cards, one pile per cause. Twelve causes. Then he lines them up, biggest first.

Look at the first two. Delivery time wrong, ninety two. Discount code refused, fifty eight. A hundred and fifty of two hundred. Three quarters.

Add the piles from the left. The line shoots up, then crawls.

So Sam fixes those two. Also one week. Next month's pile shrinks to about a quarter. The other ten would take a month.

Code is the same. Priya timed the slow menu page. Ninety percent of the wait was one job, resizing photos.

But count, don't guess. The split is rough, not always eighty twenty. And weigh harm. Two customers were charged twice. Rare, but it jumps the queue.

That is the Pareto principle. A few causes make most of the effects. So count before you choose.
