Maya asks Sam how long gift messages will take. A note from the customer, printed on a card in the box.

Sam pictures it. A box on the website. Save the note. Print the card. Two days.

Maya knows better. She doubles it. Four days.

Then the task unfolds. The box and the card take two days, as planned.

Then the odd cases. A note too long for the card. Emoji the old printer cannot print. Rude words.

Then the work around the work. The kitchen screen, which another team owns. Tests. A notice to the delivery partners. A review.

And an emergency steals two days.

Sam guessed two. Maya planned four. It took nine. Even the doubled guess was short.

So break the work into small pieces. List the work around the work. Check what similar tasks really took. Give a range, six to ten days. And when it slips, say so early.

That is Hofstadter's law. It always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter's Law.
